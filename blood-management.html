Blood Products

Packed Red Blood Cells
Composed Of:
Concentrated donor red cells with residual plasma, additive solution, and minimal platelets or coagulation factors.
MOA:
Increases hemoglobin mass and arterial oxygen-carrying capacity.
Indication:
Symptomatic anemia, oxygen-delivery limitation, or blood loss with inadequate hematocrit during CPB.
Contraindications:
No absolute contraindication when lifesaving; avoid incompatible units and use caution with prior severe transfusion reaction, TACO risk, or patient refusal.
ABO Compatibility:
Required; transfuse ABO-compatible red cells with appropriate crossmatch or emergency-release protocol.
Estimated Hematocrit Rise:
In an average adult without active bleeding or hemolysis, 1 unit typically increases hematocrit by about 3%.
Dose for Bypass:
Common adult dose is 1 unit at a time, reassessing hematocrit, oxygen delivery, volume status, and bleeding.
Adverse Reactions:
Hemolytic reaction, febrile or allergic reaction, TACO, TRALI, hyperkalemia, hypocalcemia from citrate, infection risk, and alloimmunization.
Platelets
Composed Of:
Apheresis or pooled donor platelets suspended in plasma or platelet additive solution.
MOA:
Provides functional platelets for primary hemostasis and clot surface formation.
Indication:
Thrombocytopenia or platelet dysfunction with surgical bleeding after CPB or massive transfusion.
Contraindications:
Avoid routine use in TTP, HIT, or ITP without life-threatening bleeding; use caution with prior severe platelet reaction or platelet refractoriness.
ABO Compatibility:
ABO-identical or compatible platelets are preferred, but non-identical platelets may be used when clinically necessary.
Dose for Bypass:
Common adult dose is 1 apheresis unit or equivalent pooled dose, guided by platelet count, function, and bleeding.
Adverse Reactions:
Febrile or allergic reaction, bacterial contamination, TRALI, TACO, alloimmunization, refractoriness, and thrombosis risk when overcorrected.
FFP
Composed Of:
Donor plasma containing soluble coagulation factors, fibrinogen, albumin, and anticoagulant proteins.
MOA:
Replaces multiple coagulation factors to support thrombin generation and fibrin clot formation.
Indication:
Bleeding with factor deficiency, elevated INR, massive transfusion, or coagulopathy after CPB when factor replacement is needed.
Contraindications:
Avoid for volume expansion or nutritional support; use caution with severe plasma allergy, IgA deficiency with anti-IgA, or high TACO/TRALI risk.
ABO Compatibility:
Required; use ABO-compatible plasma to avoid donor anti-A or anti-B hemolysis of recipient red cells.
Dose for Bypass:
Common adult dose is 10 to 15 mL/kg, then reassess coagulation testing and surgical bleeding.
Adverse Reactions:
Allergic reaction, TACO, TRALI, citrate-related hypocalcemia, hemolysis from incompatible plasma, and infection risk.
Cryoprecipitate
Composed Of:
Cold-insoluble plasma fraction rich in fibrinogen, factor VIII, factor XIII, von Willebrand factor, and fibronectin.
MOA:
Raises fibrinogen and supports final fibrin clot strength.
Indication:
Bleeding with low fibrinogen or poor clot firmness after CPB, massive transfusion, or hemodilution.
Contraindications:
Avoid when fibrinogen is adequate or a pathogen-reduced fibrinogen concentrate is specifically required; use caution with severe plasma allergy or thrombotic risk.
ABO Compatibility:
Not usually required because plasma volume is small, but ABO-compatible cryoprecipitate is preferred when available.
Dose for Bypass:
Common adult dose is 10 pooled units or about 1 unit per 10 kg, guided by fibrinogen or viscoelastic testing.
Adverse Reactions:
Allergic or febrile reaction, TACO, TRALI, infection risk, hemolysis risk from plasma incompatibility, and thrombosis if overcorrected.
4-Factor PCC
Composed Of:
Concentrated human vitamin K-dependent factors II, VII, IX, and X with proteins C and S, plus heparin in some products.
MOA:
Rapidly restores vitamin K-dependent coagulation factor activity and thrombin generation.
Indication:
Urgent warfarin reversal for major bleeding or urgent procedure; off-label rescue for refractory cardiac surgical coagulopathy per protocol.
Contraindications:
Known anaphylaxis to PCC components, heparin-induced thrombocytopenia when product contains heparin, or DIC unless benefits outweigh thrombosis risk.
ABO Compatibility:
Not required; PCC is a factor concentrate, not a cellular or plasma component selected by ABO type.
Dose for Bypass:
For warfarin reversal, dose by INR and weight, commonly 25 to 50 units/kg; cardiac-surgery rescue dosing is institution-specific.
Adverse Reactions:
Thromboembolism, DIC, headache, nausea, vomiting, hypotension, anemia, and hypersensitivity including heparin-related risk.
Recombinant Activated Factor 7
Composed Of:
Recombinant activated human coagulation factor VIIa.
MOA:
Enhances tissue-factor pathway activity and, at pharmacologic doses, promotes thrombin generation on activated platelets.
Indication:
Rescue therapy for severe refractory bleeding after surgical bleeding, hypothermia, acidosis, thrombocytopenia, and low fibrinogen are corrected.
Contraindications:
Hypersensitivity to rFVIIa or product components; use extreme caution with active thrombosis, recent stroke, MI, or DIC.
ABO Compatibility:
Not required; recombinant activated factor VII is not an ABO-typed blood component.
Dose for Bypass:
Cardiac-surgery rescue dosing is protocol-driven; commonly 20 to 40 mcg/kg IV once, with repeat dosing only after reassessment.
Adverse Reactions:
Arterial or venous thrombosis, stroke, myocardial infarction, pulmonary embolism, DIC, hypersensitivity, and inhibitor development.

Blood Management Strategies

Bleeding Management
Modified Ultrafiltration:
May be used after CPB to help reduce circulating anticoagulant effect and manage excessive bleeding in select cases.
Hemodialysis:
May be considered when anticoagulant effect is prolonged, especially with renal impairment or dialyzable anticoagulants.
Recombinant Factor VIIa:
May be considered as rescue therapy for severe refractory bleeding or coagulopathy, guided by institutional protocol.
FFP:
Adjunctive hemostatic support to replace coagulation factors during balanced resuscitation.
Cryoprecipitate:
Adjunctive support for fibrinogen replacement when fibrinogen is low or bleeding is consistent with hypofibrinogenemia.
Platelets:
Adjunctive support for thrombocytopenia or platelet dysfunction after CPB when clinically indicated.
TXA
MOA:
Synthetic lysine analog that blocks plasminogen binding to fibrin, reducing plasmin formation and fibrinolysis.
Dose:
Common CPB range: 10 to 30 mg/kg IV load, 1 to 16 mg/kg/hr infusion, and 1 to 2 mg/kg in the pump prime per protocol.
Use:
Blood-conservation strategy to reduce CPB-associated fibrinolysis and perioperative bleeding.
AT3
MOA:
Restores antithrombin activity, potentiating heparin inhibition of thrombin and factor Xa.
Indication:
AT deficiency or suspected heparin resistance during CPB, guided by ACT, heparin concentration, and local protocol.
Dose on Bypass:
Common protocol dose is 500 to 1,000 units IV; label-based dosing targets AT activity using units = (target % - baseline %) × weight kg / 1.4.
Contraindications:
Hypersensitivity to antithrombin product components; use caution with active bleeding and concurrent anticoagulation.
ABO Compatibility:
Not required; AT3 is a plasma-derived or recombinant concentrate, not an ABO-typed blood component.
Adverse Drug Reactions:
Bleeding, hypersensitivity, dizziness, chest discomfort, nausea, dysgeusia, cramps or pain, and infection risk with plasma-derived products.
EACA
MOA:
Synthetic lysine analog that inhibits plasminogen activation and plasmin activity, stabilizing fibrin clot.
Dose:
Common CPB range: 100 to 150 mg/kg IV load, then 10 to 30 mg/kg/hr or 1 g/hr infusion; some protocols add pump-prime dosing.
Use:
Blood-conservation strategy to reduce CPB-associated fibrinolysis and perioperative bleeding.

Educational and simulation support only. Confirm transfusion thresholds, product availability, and blood-management protocols against local policy before use.
