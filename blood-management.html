Blood Products

Packed Red Blood Cells
Composed Of:
Concentrated donor red cells with residual plasma, additive solution, and minimal platelets or coagulation factors.
MOA:
Increases hemoglobin mass and arterial oxygen-carrying capacity.
Indication:
Symptomatic anemia, oxygen-delivery limitation, or blood loss with inadequate hematocrit during CPB.
Dose for Bypass:
Common adult dose is 1 unit at a time, reassessing hematocrit, oxygen delivery, volume status, and bleeding.
Adverse Reactions:
Hemolytic reaction, febrile or allergic reaction, TACO, TRALI, hyperkalemia, hypocalcemia from citrate, infection risk, and alloimmunization.
Platelets
Composed Of:
Apheresis or pooled donor platelets suspended in plasma or platelet additive solution.
MOA:
Provides functional platelets for primary hemostasis and clot surface formation.
Indication:
Thrombocytopenia or platelet dysfunction with surgical bleeding after CPB or massive transfusion.
Dose for Bypass:
Common adult dose is 1 apheresis unit or equivalent pooled dose, guided by platelet count, function, and bleeding.
Adverse Reactions:
Febrile or allergic reaction, bacterial contamination, TRALI, TACO, alloimmunization, refractoriness, and thrombosis risk when overcorrected.
FFP
Composed Of:
Donor plasma containing soluble coagulation factors, fibrinogen, albumin, and anticoagulant proteins.
MOA:
Replaces multiple coagulation factors to support thrombin generation and fibrin clot formation.
Indication:
Bleeding with factor deficiency, elevated INR, massive transfusion, or coagulopathy after CPB when factor replacement is needed.
Dose for Bypass:
Common adult dose is 10 to 15 mL/kg, then reassess coagulation testing and surgical bleeding.
Adverse Reactions:
Allergic reaction, TACO, TRALI, citrate-related hypocalcemia, hemolysis from incompatible plasma, and infection risk.
Cryoprecipitate
Composed Of:
Cold-insoluble plasma fraction rich in fibrinogen, factor VIII, factor XIII, von Willebrand factor, and fibronectin.
MOA:
Raises fibrinogen and supports final fibrin clot strength.
Indication:
Bleeding with low fibrinogen or poor clot firmness after CPB, massive transfusion, or hemodilution.
Dose for Bypass:
Common adult dose is 10 pooled units or about 1 unit per 10 kg, guided by fibrinogen or viscoelastic testing.
Adverse Reactions:
Allergic or febrile reaction, TACO, TRALI, infection risk, hemolysis risk from plasma incompatibility, and thrombosis if overcorrected.
4-Factor PCC
Composed Of:
Concentrated human vitamin K-dependent factors II, VII, IX, and X with proteins C and S, plus heparin in some products.
MOA:
Rapidly restores vitamin K-dependent coagulation factor activity and thrombin generation.
Indication:
Urgent warfarin reversal for major bleeding or urgent procedure; off-label rescue for refractory cardiac surgical coagulopathy per protocol.
Dose for Bypass:
For warfarin reversal, dose by INR and weight, commonly 25 to 50 units/kg; cardiac-surgery rescue dosing is institution-specific.
Adverse Reactions:
Thromboembolism, DIC, headache, nausea, vomiting, hypotension, anemia, and hypersensitivity including heparin-related risk.
Recombinant Activated Factor 7
Composed Of:
Recombinant activated human coagulation factor VIIa.
MOA:
Enhances tissue-factor pathway activity and, at pharmacologic doses, promotes thrombin generation on activated platelets.
Indication:
Rescue therapy for severe refractory bleeding after surgical bleeding, hypothermia, acidosis, thrombocytopenia, and low fibrinogen are corrected.
Dose for Bypass:
Cardiac-surgery rescue dosing is protocol-driven; commonly 20 to 40 mcg/kg IV once, with repeat dosing only after reassessment.
Adverse Reactions:
Arterial or venous thrombosis, stroke, myocardial infarction, pulmonary embolism, DIC, hypersensitivity, and inhibitor development.
